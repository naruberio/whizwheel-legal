Whizwheel for iPhone and iPad · last revised 2026-10-23

Whizwheel opens on a board of fourteen tools, with About at the end. Tap one, type the figures you already have, and the answer updates with every keystroke. Nothing needs a connection, so it behaves the same on the ramp and in airplane mode.

The board

COPYWrite down the ATIS (letter, time, wind, gusts, temperature, dew point, altimeter setting, runway in use), the airport identifier and field elevation, and the hold instructions (fix, inbound course, turns, leg time, EFC) once, then use them in DA, RWY and HOLD.
WINDWind correction angle, heading and ground speed; or the wind itself from what you flew. Runway components. Add the magnetic variation and it also gives the magnetic course and heading.
TASCalibrated, true and equivalent airspeed and Mach.
DAPressure and density altitude, plus the cold-temperature correction and true altitude. Enter an airport identifier to check it against the FAA list of cold temperature airports bundled with the App: its restricted temperature and the segments marked on the list, and, when the field temperature you enter is at or below the restricted temperature, the correction for each segment altitude you enter.
TSDTime, speed or distance from the other two.
TIMEAdding and subtracting flight times in hours, minutes and seconds.
FUELFuel required for a given time, endurance, and what the fuel weighs.
W&BMoment, centre of gravity and limits, with aircraft profiles you can save.
RWYTakeoff and landing distances over a 50 ft obstacle from the tables you copy from your POH into a saved aircraft profile, with the wind note under each table. They are compared with the runway length, the crosswind with the max demonstrated crosswind, and the takeoff weight and CG with the profile's limits. Corrections for runway surface and slope are not included, and the screen does not tell you whether you may take off or land.
DESWhere to start down and the descent rate, from a gradient in ft/nm, % or degrees; the climb rate a SID or missed-approach gradient asks for.
HOLDWhich holding entry to fly: direct, teardrop or parallel. Then, from the wind at the holding altitude: the inbound and outbound headings, the outbound time, one circuit, and how many full circuits fit before the EFC.
PNRPoint of no return and equal time point, with different ground speeds out and back.
1:60From distance flown, distance off course and distance remaining: track error, closing angle and the turn to fly direct, solved with trigonometry rather than the 1-in-60 shortcut.
CONVDistance, speed, weight, volume, pressure and temperature.

Moving a figure to the next tool

Some answers are the input of the next problem. When one is available, the next tool shows a button naming the value and the tool it came from. Tap it to fill the field; until you do, nothing is changed for you. The six hand-offs are:

  • pressure altitude (and temperature, if entered) from DA to TAS
  • true airspeed from TAS to WIND (both the wind triangle and finding the wind from what you flew)
  • true airspeed from TAS to HOLD
  • ground speed from WIND to TSD
  • leg time from TSD to FUEL
  • the profile, takeoff weight and CG from W&B to RWY

Each hand-off holds only its most recent value (for DA to TAS and W&B to RWY, the most recent set), and all of them are gone when the App is closed.

What you write in COPY is offered the same way, as a button you tap:

  • to DA: the field elevation and altimeter setting (and temperature) for pressure altitude, the elevation and temperature for the cold-temperature correction, and the airport identifier for the cold temperature airport list
  • to RWY: pressure altitude from the field elevation and altimeter setting, the temperature, the runway in use and the ATIS wind with gusts
  • to HOLD: the inbound course and turns, the leg time and the EFC

The ATIS wind is the surface wind, so it goes to RWY only. WIND and HOLD use the wind aloft. Unlike the hand-offs, the copy is kept on the device; see the privacy notice.

Typing a time

The time fields in TSD, FUEL and PNR read a plain number as minutes: 45 is 45 minutes and 8.5 is 8 minutes 30 seconds. A colon switches to hours: 8:56 is 8 hours 56 minutes, so 8 minutes 56 seconds is 0:08:56. Under the field the App spells out what it understood, so a slip shows straight away.

The TIME tool, which adds or subtracts two flight times, reads a plain number differently: there it is hours (1.5 is 1:30:00), and minutes take a colon (0:45). Anything written with a colon means the same in all of these fields.

The EFC (in COPY and HOLD) and the time at the fix (in HOLD) are different: they are UTC times of day, written hh:mm or hhmm, so 1430 and 14:30 are both 14:30 UTC.

When an answer differs from yours

Where pilots use a cockpit rule of thumb, Whizwheel solves the underlying equation instead, so an answer can differ from that rule and from a mechanical E6B, by an amount that depends on the conditions. Two examples: density altitude comes from the standard atmosphere rather than 120 ft per degree above ISA, and at 5,000 ft pressure altitude and 30 °C the two are about 190 ft apart; true airspeed includes compressibility rather than 2% per thousand feet, and at 250 kt calibrated, FL250 and −35 °C the rule comes out about 12 kt higher. The same goes for the cold-temperature correction, solved with the ICAO Doc 8168 equation, and for 1:60, solved with trigonometry. Fuel weight is the exception: it uses a fixed typical density for each fuel type, not the temperature of your fuel, so a fuel slip may show a slightly different weight.

The ICAO cold-temperature table (also printed as AIM Table 7-3-1) is worked out for a sea-level aerodrome. Whizwheel works it out for the elevation you enter instead, so at an aerodrome above sea level its correction can be smaller than the table's. When the aerodrome temperature is at or above ISA for that elevation, Whizwheel applies no correction at all, even where the table still lists one.

If you think an answer is wrong, please write to us with the tool, every figure on the screen, and where your own figure came from (a POH table, a mechanical E6B, a test question), so we can work through the same problem.

Aircraft profiles

Aircraft profiles, with the weight-and-balance stations and limits you save in W&B and the distance tables you copy in RWY, live in Whizwheel's storage on this device. Whizwheel never syncs or sends them anywhere, and removing the App removes them. The tables are only as right as your copy of the POH, so check them against the POH after saving. The privacy notice lists what a profile holds and how an iOS device backup may include it.

Knowledge tests

Phones and tablets stay outside the FAA testing room, so Whizwheel cannot go in with you. Take an approved flight computer, or use the one the test centre provides.

What it leaves out

No maps, weather, navigation log or flight plan filing. Keeping to calculation is why it needs no network, asks for no permissions and is a single purchase.
